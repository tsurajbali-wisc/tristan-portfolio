A — OVERVIEW

Unlike PDC and lighting, this one's fully mine.

Where the PDC and lighting boards involved inherited architecture, this is an independent project: I designed the schematic, laid out the PCB, and brought up the hardware end to end.

The goal was a small closed-loop controller — read temperature, drive a fan proportionally, and protect itself if something goes wrong electrically — built around an STM32 rather than an off-the-shelf fan controller.

FIG. 01 — PENDINGSystem block diagram: 12 V input → protection → buck stage → STM32 → PWM driver → fan
B — DESIGN

Four subsystems on one small board.

INPUT

Protected 12 V input

Input protection ahead of the regulator so a reversed or noisy 12 V supply doesn't take out the rest of the board.

POWER

Buck regulation

Steps the 12 V input down to logic voltage for the STM32 and sensing circuitry.

SENSING

Temperature sensing

Feeds live temperature readings to the STM32, which sets fan speed based on measured conditions rather than a fixed duty cycle.

DRIVE

MOSFET PWM control

Drives the 6 W brushless fan with a MOSFET switching stage under STM32 PWM control, with current fault detection to catch stall or overcurrent conditions.

C — VALIDATION

Bring-up on the bench, one subsystem at a time.

I validated the power, PWM, and sensing circuitry independently during bring-up: checking regulator output and stability, verifying PWM waveforms into the MOSFET stage, and confirming the temperature-to-speed response, using an oscilloscope to check switching behavior and signal integrity along the way.
